Step 3: Set Minimum Confluence Score — how many strategies must agree. Higher = fewer but stronger signals.

Backtesting Engine Guide
Test 40 strategies against historical data
✕ Close
Before risking real money, test your strategy against actual historical data. The engine runs your chosen strategy on any NSE stock over any time period, simulating trades with realistic slippage, and produces a comprehensive performance report with 15+ risk metrics.
📌 How to Use — Step by Step
Step 1: Enter a stock symbol (e.g., RELIANCE, TCS, INFY).
Step 2: Select a strategy — 39 options across Technical (SMA Crossover, MACD, Supertrend, Bollinger, Breakout), Value (Low P/E, Deep Value, GARP), Quality (Piotroski, High ROE, Low Debt), Growth (EPS Growth, Revenue), and Hybrid (Growth+Breakout, Quality+MACD).
Step 3: Set time period (start/end dates) and initial capital.
Step 4: Configure exits — Stop Loss %, Target %, and advanced exits (ATR trailing, time-based, R-multiple).
Step 5: Click RUN BACKTEST. View equity curve, trade log, and risk metrics.
📈 Equity Curve
Visual chart showing how capital grows over time. Steadily rising with shallow drawdowns = good strategy. Compared against buy-and-hold benchmark.
📊 Key Metrics
Total Return, CAGR, Win Rate, Sharpe Ratio (above 1.0 is good), Max Drawdown, Sortino Ratio, Calmar Ratio, Avg Win vs Avg Loss, Profit Factor.
Investors: Validate strategy before deploying capital. Test across market cycles to understand drawdown risk.  |  Advisors: Evidence-backed recommendations — "this strategy returned 24% CAGR with 1.3 Sharpe" carries weight with clients.

ARBITRAGE
Cash-Futures spread trading — low-risk market-neutral opportunities
The Arbitrage scanner identifies price differences between a stock's cash (spot) and futures price. When futures trade at a premium to spot, you can lock in a near risk-free return by buying cash and selling futures simultaneously.
How to Use:
1. Ensure Kite (Zerodha) is connected via Settings → Kite Login
2. Go to ARBITRAGE tab — scanner runs automatically
3. Stocks are sorted by Annualized Return % (highest first)
4. Look for stocks with Annualized > 8% and signal "STRONG" or "GOOD"
5. Check Net Return (after costs) — must be higher than FD rate (~7%) to be worthwhile
6. AVOID stocks with negative basis (backwardation) — you'd lock in a loss

Reading the Table:
• Basis: Futures Price − Spot Price (positive = contango = opportunity)
• Basis %: Basis as percentage of spot price
• Annualized: Basis % projected to a full year
• Net Return: Annualized return after all transaction costs
• Signal: STRONG (>12%), GOOD (>8%), FAIR (>5%), AVOID (<-2%)
💡 Example: RELIANCE Spot ₹1,413, Futures ₹1,421. Basis = ₹8 (0.57%). 25 days to expiry. Annualized = 8.3%. Lot size 250. You buy 250 shares in cash (₹3.53L) and sell 1 lot futures. At expiry, both converge. Gross profit: ₹2,000. After costs (~₹350): Net ₹1,650.

JOBBING
High-frequency bid-ask spread trading — many small trades per day
Jobbing (also called market making) involves making 20-50 small trades per day, profiting from the bid-ask spread. The scanner scores F&O stocks on spread tightness, volatility, price level, and day range to identify the best candidates.
How to Read the Scanner:
• Score (0-10): Higher = better candidate. 7+ = STRONG, 5+ = GOOD
• Spread %: Cash-futures spread. Tighter (<0.05%) = lower cost per trade
• Day Range %: Intraday volatility. Sweet spot: 0.5%-2.0%
• Cost/Trade: Total round-trip cost (brokerage + STT + charges)
• Target Profit/Lot: Expected profit per trade at 0.05% target move
• Breakeven Move: Minimum price move needed to cover costs

Key Rule: Only trade stocks where cost per trade < 50% of target profit. If costs eat more than half your target, it's not viable.

Best Windows: 09:15-10:00 (opening volatility) and 14:30-15:15 (closing rebalancing). Avoid 12:00-14:00 (low volume lunch period).
⚠️ Warning: Jobbing requires very fast execution, discipline, and experience. Transaction costs can quickly accumulate. Always paper trade for at least 2-4 weeks before attempting real jobbing. Use the Calculator tab to verify your expected daily P&L is positive after all costs.

SCALPING
Directional momentum trades — 5-10 trades per day targeting 0.3%-1% moves
Unlike jobbing (non-directional), scalping takes a directional bet — BULLISH or BEARISH — based on VWAP deviation, day change momentum, and price pattern. Fewer trades but larger profit per trade.
How to Read the Scanner:
• Direction: BULLISH (green), BEARISH (red), NEUTRAL (grey) — determined by day change + VWAP deviation
• VWAP Dev: How far price is from VWAP. Positive = above VWAP (bullish). Negative = below (bearish)
• Pattern: NEAR HIGH, NEAR LOW, MID RANGE — where price sits in today's range
• Entry/Target/Stop: Pre-calculated levels. Bullish: target +0.5%, stop -0.3%. Bearish: reversed
• R:R Ratio: Reward-to-Risk. Above 1.5:1 is good. This means you make 1.5× what you risk
• Pivot Levels: S2/S1/Pivot/R1/R2 — additional reference for support/resistance

How to Trade:
1. Filter for STRONG signal (score ≥ 7) in your preferred direction
2. Confirm VWAP deviation aligns with direction (>0.5% for conviction)
3. Enter at Entry price, set Stop and Target as shown
4. Exit at target or stop — no exceptions. Discipline is everything in scalping.
💡 Example: HDFCBANK showing BULLISH, Score 7.5, VWAP Dev +0.8%, Day Change +1.2%. Entry ₹1,655, Target ₹1,663 (+0.5%), Stop ₹1,650 (-0.3%). Lot 550. Potential profit: ₹4,400. Potential loss: ₹2,750. R:R = 1.6:1. Net after costs: ~₹4,305 win / ₹2,845 loss.

DCF CALCULATOR
Discounted Cash Flow intrinsic value estimator — 3 valuation methods
Estimate a stock's fair (intrinsic) value using three independent methods: EPS-based DCF, Free Cash Flow DCF, and Graham Number. Compare against the current market price to determine if a stock is undervalued, fairly valued, or overvalued.
How to Use:
1. Enter stock symbol → Click Calculate
2. Default assumptions auto-populate (Growth: auto-detected from earnings, Discount: 12%, Terminal: 3%, Years: 10)
3. Adjust assumptions if needed — higher growth rate = higher intrinsic value
4. Review three valuations: EPS DCF, FCF DCF, Graham Number
5. Check Margin of Safety % — positive means stock is below fair value
6. Verdict: UNDERVALUED (margin > 15%), FAIRLY VALUED (margin > -10%), OVERVALUED (margin ≤ -10%)

Key Assumptions:
• Growth Rate: Annual earnings growth expected for next 10 years (auto-detected, 5%-25%)
• Discount Rate: 12% (India's cost of equity — the minimum return you'd accept)
• Terminal Growth: 3% (long-term GDP growth — what happens after year 10)
💡 Example: RELIANCE: EPS ₹61.49, auto-detected growth 10%. EPS DCF intrinsic value: ₹1,800. FCF DCF: ₹1,650. Graham Number: ₹1,720. Average: ₹1,723. Current price: ₹1,413. Margin of Safety: +18%. Verdict: UNDERVALUED. This suggests RELIANCE may be trading below its fair value — but always verify assumptions and do additional research before acting.
